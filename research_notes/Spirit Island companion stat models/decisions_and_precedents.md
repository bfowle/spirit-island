Scope: (1) a decision taxonomy for one Spirit Island round, naming the quantitative question, the uncertainty, and the model type for each decision point; (2) a survey of fair-play companion and odds tools in other games, how they display numbers, how communities judge them, and what the learning research says about display design. ## Key Question 1: Spirit Island turn structure, decision points, and the math question each raises

### Takeaway
Spirit Island has no dice. Every effect inside a round (Ravage, Build, damage, Defend, Fear accrual) follows deterministic rules from public board state. All the uncertainty comes from hidden decks: the next Explore card, Fear-card identities, Events, Minor and Major Power draws, and the Blight card's flip side. So most in-round decisions are deterministic optimization problems: forecast, knapsack, and graph search. Probability only matters for questions about next round and later, which card counting within a stage answers. Most of the time, the companion's job is to show exact consequences and "what changed and why." Hypergeometric or uniform-over-unseen odds apply only at the deck boundary.

### Cited Findings

**Phase structure (base game)**
- A round has five phases: Spirit Phase, Fast Power Phase, Invader Phase, Slow Power Phase, Time Passes. Players act simultaneously within each phase. — [RulesPal rulebook transcription](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Growth: "Choose one option (unless stated otherwise) next to 'Growth' at the upper-right of the Spirit Panel." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Gain Energy: "Gain an amount of Energy equal to the highest uncovered number on your Energy Presence Track." Card plays are capped by the highest uncovered number on the Card Plays track, even when you have more Energy. Track benefits are not additive. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Play and pay: "You must immediately pay Energy for all Power Cards played, even Slow ones." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Fast Power Phase: "You cannot delay using a Fast Power until the Slow Phase, even if you want to. Use it now or forego it." Slow Powers resolve the same way after the Invader Phase. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Base Invader Phase order: Blighted Island (if the Blight Card has flipped, follow its instructions), then Fear (earned Fear cards are flipped and resolved "in the order they were earned"), then Invader Actions (Ravage, Build, Explore), then Advance Invader Cards. Advancing slides the cards left: Ravage goes to discard, Build goes to Ravage, Explore goes to Build. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Branch & Claw Event step: a review lists the Invader Phase as Healthy/Blighted Island card triggers, then "Event Card draw and resolution," then Earned Fear Cards, then Ravage, Build, Explore. It says events "add an additional step to the Invader phase." Events have several effects; the first is often a choice (for example, *Lesser Spirits Imperiled*). — [Way Too Many Games review of Branch & Claw](https://waytoomany.games/2023/02/20/spirit-island-branch-claw-is-a-small-expansion-with-a-big-impact). This is a secondary source; the primary Branch & Claw rulebook text was not reachable (see Gaps).
- Time Passes: "Players discard all Power Cards played this turn into their personal discard piles." All Damage is cleared and all Elements go away. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Invader deck (the main hidden-deck uncertainty)**
- The deck has 12 cards: 3 Stage I on top, 4 Stage II in the middle, 5 Stage III at the bottom. "Mix up the cards from each Stage and choose without looking at them." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Setup: "You randomly return back to the box 1 card from each Tier," then stack Tier I on Tier II on Tier III. — [Dized rules FAQ: How do I create an Invader deck](https://rules.dized.com/game/_7rPptSyRn6_unBi4-2eVg/sjhFSW3wTyKIdbBw1Uc9sg/how-do-i-create-an-invader-deck)
- The box holds 15 Invader cards: 4 Stage I, 5 Stage II, 6 Stage III. This fits a 12-card deck with one card removed per stage. — [GTG Print-and-Play rules PDF](https://forum.greaterthangames.com/system/files/downloads/Spirit%20Island%20PnP%20Rules%20-%20Kickstarter.pdf) (search snippet; direct download failed)
- Every Stage III card shows two terrain types. — [Dized FAQ: Why do tier 3 cards show two terrain types](https://rules.dized.com/game/_7rPptSyRn6_unBi4-2eVg/AmDFx_t7TdStQfZyFxHtSg/why-do-tier-3-cards-show-two-terrain-types) (search snippet)
- Explore (wiki revision 12144, verbatim): "Add an Explorer to every land of the shown type which either: Contains a Town or City; or Is adjacent to a Town, City, or Ocean. No matter how many sources are in or adjacent to an Explored land, you only add one Explorer." If there is no card to turn up, "the spirits lose the game." On a flag-icon card with an Adversary, the Escalation effect happens first. — [Spirit Island Wiki, Sequence of Play §3c](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Sequence_of_Play) (local verbatim copy; live page behind Cloudflare). Explorers are not themselves an Explore source. — [Spirit Island Wiki, Explorer](https://spiritislandwiki.com/index.php?title=Explorer) (same local copy)
- Build: "If the land has more Towns than Cities, add a City." "In all other cases, add a Town." Build happens only in lands that already have Invaders. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Ravage, Defend, Dahan, blight (deterministic within the round)**
- "Wherever there are Invaders in the shown lands, Invaders deal Damage: 1 per Explorer, 2 per Town, and 3 per City." "If 2 or more Damage is dealt, add a Blight to the land." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Dahan have 2 Health. "Every 2 points of Damage destroys one Dahan." "You must destroy Dahan as efficiently as possible; you cannot disperse Damage among multiple Dahan to avoid killing them." Surviving Dahan then deal 2 Damage each back to the Invaders. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Cascade: "if that land already has Blight, you must also add a Blight to one adjacent land." Adding Blight destroys one Presence of each Spirit in that land. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Defend reduces Invader Damage in a land this turn. Multiple Defends stack. Example: Defend 2 means "reduce the total amount of Damage they do by 2." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- When a Spirit Power deals Damage to Invaders, it is "divided among them however you choose." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Fear and victory**
- "destroying a Town generates 1 Fear and destroying a City generates 2 Fear." Setup: "Put 4 Fear Markers per player into the Fear Pool." When the pool fills, "move the top card of the Fear Deck face-down into the Earned Fear Cards area." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Removing or replacing an Invader generates no Fear; only destroying it does. — [Dized FAQ: removing vs. destroying](https://rules.dized.com/game/_7rPptSyRn6_unBi4-2eVg/1l8Yps-FRee-H8YMbqCOQw/what-is-the-difference-between-removing-and-destroying-an-invader) (search snippet)
- Victory by Terror Level: TL1, no Invaders on the island. TL2, no Towns or Cities. TL3, no Cities. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Defeat: the Blight Card runs out (handled per the card's text), any Spirit has no Presence on the island, or the Invader Deck is empty when you must draw to Explore. Destroyed Presence is set beside the island, not returned to the panel. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Powers, elements, innates**
- "Innate Powers have Elemental Thresholds, meaning they can only be used on Turns you have all the required Elements." "Elements are never spent, only checked." An innate fires only once even with twice the elements. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Power Card elements count from the moment the card is paid for, "regardless of whether the Power is Fast or Slow." So a Slow card's elements already count toward Fast innates. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Gaining a Power Card: draw 4 from the chosen deck, keep 1, discard the rest to that deck's discard. "After you gain a Major Power, you must Forget - permanently lose - one of your Power Cards." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Reclaim returns all played Power Cards from your discard to your hand. Presence is added "at Range 2 (up to 2 lands away from your existing Presence)." A Sacred Site is a land with 2 or more of a Spirit's Presence. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)
- Gather: "Move that many things into the target land from land(s) adjacent to it." Push: "Move that many things out of the target land, to adjacent land(s)." — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook)

**Tokens (Branch & Claw; secondary description)**
- Disease blocks the next Build in its land and is then removed. Wilds blocks the next Explore and is then removed. Strife is placed under one Invader, which then deals no damage during Ravage. Beasts are used by Event and Power cards. — [Way Too Many Games review](https://waytoomany.games/2023/02/20/spirit-island-branch-claw-is-a-small-expansion-with-a-big-impact). These are a reviewer's paraphrases; verify exact Strife and Beasts text before modeling.

**Existing Spirit Island helpers**
- *Spirit Guide* (Nerd Room Software) offers "Energy / Element Trackers," "Notifications when innate abilities can be performed," a setup guide, a randomizer, and a "Scoring calculator." It tracks state and computes no probabilities. — [game-solver.com/spirit-guide](https://game-solver.com/spirit-guide/); [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spirit-guide/id1452929632). One App Store reviewer reported wrong Fear-card counts for level-2 adversaries. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/spirit-guide/id1452929632) (search snippet)
- The Tabletop Simulator Spirit Island mod has an "Explore" button that flips the top Invader card. It automates the game rather than advising. — [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/2104364163?p=6) (search snippet)
- No public Spirit Island tool was found that computes Explore, Ravage, or power-draw odds (see Gaps).

### Inferences

**I-1. Two uncertainty regimes.** Within a round, once the Ravage and Build cards are face-up, outcomes in those lands follow fixed rules. The rules above contain no random step in Ravage, Build, Defend, Dahan counterattack, blight, or Fear accrual. Randomness enters only through draws from hidden decks: the Invader deck (next Explore), the Fear deck (identity of earned cards, hidden face-down until resolved), the Event deck, the Minor and Major Power decks, and the Blight card's other side. The companion's stats should therefore split into *deterministic forecasts* (exact, no `approx` flag except for unmodeled adversary or token rules) and *deck-odds* (uniform over unseen cards in the relevant stage or pool).

**I-2. Invader-deck odds from card counting (rules-derived, needs composition verified).** If the composition is 4 / 5 / 6 cards per stage (box) with one removed per stage, and Stage III cards are the six two-terrain pairs of four terrains (C(4,2) = 6, which matches the six-card count):
- Stage I, nothing seen: P(next Explore = a given terrain) = 1/4. After one Stage I card is seen it is 1/3 for each unseen terrain, and after two it is 1/2. The pocket rule is **"1 ÷ unseen cards left in this stage"**, the Spirit Island counterpart of poker's "rule of 2 and 4."
- Stage III, nothing seen: each terrain is on 3 of the 6 cards, so P(next card hits a given terrain) = 3/6 = 50%. In general it is (unseen cards in this stage that show terrain T) ÷ (unseen cards in this stage).
- Stage II: if it holds four single-terrain Escalation cards plus "Coastal Lands," each starts at 1/5. This composition was not verified from a primary source (see Gaps).
- The card back shows its stage, so the stage of the next card is known (spec Section 4). The 1-of-N removal per stage is exactly why the spec forbids counting the save's actual deck.
- Per-land P(explored next round) = P(next card matches the land's terrain, or Coastal for coastal lands) × [the land is an Explore source-target: it has a Town or City, or is adjacent to a Town, City, or Ocean]. The second factor is deterministic.

**I-3. Ravage arithmetic for one land (deterministic).** Let D = 1·E + 2·T + 3·C (Explorers, Towns, Cities) and d = Defend in that land. The rules above give:
- Blight is added iff D − d ≥ 2, so Defend needed to prevent blight = max(0, D − 1).
- Dahan destroyed = min(#Dahan, ⌊(D − d)/2⌋). Counterattack = 2 × surviving Dahan.
- If the land already has blight, a blight added here cascades to one adjacent land and destroys Presence there. That makes "expected blight prevented" equal to 1 or 2+ (with cascade), not 1.
- Adversary rules, tokens (Strife, Badlands, Beasts), and spirit specials change this. The spec already plans to mark those as `approx`.

**I-4. Decision taxonomy.** The table below synthesizes the rules above. "Model" means the computation that answers the question; "Display" is a suggestion for the companion, informed by Key Question 3.

| # | Decision point (phase) | Quantitative question | Uncertainty | Model type | Learning-oriented display |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Growth option** (Spirit Phase) | Which option maximizes resources over the next k rounds: energy per round, card plays, presence on board, reclaim timing? When does this option "pay back" against the alternatives? | Mostly deterministic (your own tracks). Power-draw options add deck uncertainty (row 9). | Deterministic multi-round projection (tempo or energy curve); small DP or beam search over growth sequences | Side-by-side tempo curves ("Option A: +1 E/round from round 4; Option B: reclaim now, 2 extra card plays next round") with the assumptions listed |
| 2 | **Presence placement** (Growth) | Which land at Range ≤2 maximizes future targeting reach, sacred sites, and element or track value, and minimizes destruction risk (blight added destroys Presence)? | Deterministic for this round's Ravage card. Probabilistic for later rounds via the Explore → Build → Ravage pipeline | Graph search (BFS ranges on adjacency) plus per-land risk: P(land is ravaged within n rounds), built from invader-deck counting (I-2) and the Explore source rule | Per-land "risk in 1/2/3 rounds" badge plus "lands reachable at Range 1/2 from here" |
| 3 | **Which power cards to play** (Spirit Phase) | Under the energy budget and card-play cap, which subset maximizes value this round: blight prevented, Fear, Invaders removed, element thresholds met? What does it cost in future flexibility (reclaim cycle)? | Deterministic this round (hand, energy, and the visible Build/Ravage cards are all known) | Constrained combinatorial optimization: knapsack on energy × card plays, plus set cover for element thresholds; enumerate (hands are small) | "Legal plays" list with the energy and element math shown; flag the dominated plays and say why, not just "best" |
| 4 | **Innate threshold** (Fast/Slow phase) | Do my elements (presence track + all cards paid this turn) reach tier n? What is the cheapest card set that does? Is the next tier worth an extra card play? | Deterministic this turn. Future turns depend on draws and reclaims | Element-count check (exact); min-cost subset for each tier | Threshold bars: "have 2 Fire / need 3; cheapest fix: Card X (1E)". Mind the phase rule: Slow-card elements count for Fast innates. |
| 5 | **Targeting a land** (Fast or Slow) | Which land gives the most blight prevented, Dahan saved, Fear gained, and future builds denied? Ravage land now vs. Build land now vs. next round's likely Explore | Deterministic for the face-up Ravage and Build cards. Probabilistic for the Explore after next (I-2) | Deterministic Ravage/Build simulator per land with and without the power; difference = value. Next-round value weighted by Explore probability. | "What changes if you hit land X": a before/after delta of blight, Dahan, Fear, Invaders. This is the core teaching delta. |
| 6 | **Damage assignment** (resolving a power) | Given N damage, which Invaders to destroy to minimize the next Ravage's damage, prevent this Ravage's blight, and maximize Fear (Town 1, City 2)? | Deterministic | Small integer optimization over Invader health (needs a citation; see Gaps). Compare "kill City" vs. "kill Town + Explorer" for damage removed vs. Fear gained. | Show the 2–3 Pareto choices: damage-removed vs. Fear vs. blight-prevented |
| 7 | **Defend vs. removal** | Defend d prevents blight only if d ≥ D − 1 this turn. Removal persists, so how many future Ravages and Builds in that land does it prevent? | This turn deterministic. Future value depends on whether the land's terrain recurs (I-2) | Deterministic one-round Ravage comparison plus a multi-round expectation of recurrence | "Defend 3 stops this blight (needs ≥3). Destroying the Town instead also stops it AND removes 2 dmg/ravage for N future rounds (P recur = …)" |
| 8 | **Generate Fear vs. destroy Invaders** | How many rounds closer to the next Terror Level or Fear card does this play bring me, vs. how much Ravage damage and Build potential does it remove? How does each move the win/loss race? | Fear-card *effects* are hidden until resolved (earned face-down). Fear *count* math is exact. | Race model: projected victory round (Fear rate, Terror Level victory condition) vs. projected loss round (blight rate, invader deck exhaustion). Treat Fear-card value as a generic per-card average; the specific earned card cannot be known. | "Fear to next card: 3. This card brings TL2 one round earlier; TL2 win condition needs X towns/cities removed." |
| 9 | **Minor vs. Major power gain** (Growth) | P(the draw of 4 contains ≥1 card with element E, cost ≤ c, or speed Fast)? Expected best-of-4 value? Major cost: a forgotten card, higher energy cost, rounds of remaining game to amortize | Hidden deck. Uniform over (allowed pool − cards seen or held) | Hypergeometric: P(≥1 of K in 4 draws from N) = 1 − C(N−K,4)/C(N,4). Illustration only, pool numbers not real: N=50, K=20 gives 1 − 27,405/230,300 ≈ 88%. The best-of-4 order statistic needs card values. | Show the odds as a natural frequency ("about 9 in 10 draws give a Water card") with the pool size behind it |
| 10 | **Which card to forget** (Major gain) | Which card's loss costs least: elements toward innate thresholds, energy efficiency, unique effects (speed, range, Defend)? | Deterministic (your own cards) | Deterministic marginal-value ranking. Recompute innate tier coverage without each card | "Forgetting X drops Innate tier 2 coverage from 3 of 4 cycles to 1 of 4" |
| 11 | **Fast vs. Slow timing** (via card choice; the speed is printed) | Fast acts before this round's Ravage/Build/Explore and can prevent blight now. Slow acts after Explore, so you see where Explorers landed but this round's Ravage is already done. What is the value of that information against the value of acting earlier? | Fast: deterministic this-round benefit. Slow: removes Explore uncertainty (the Explore card flips before Slow) | Expected value of information: compare Fast value now with Slow value after the Explore draw, averaged over the possible Explore cards (I-2) | "Fast now prevents 1 blight. Slow later: 50% the Explore hits Jungle, where this power would also stop next round's Build." |
| 12 | **Push/Gather destinations** | Where do moved Invaders or Dahan do the least harm or the most good: avoid lands matching the face-up Build and Ravage cards, avoid feeding Explore sources (Towns/Cities), Gather Dahan to reach counterattack thresholds? | Deterministic vs. face-up cards. Probabilistic vs. the next Explore (I-2) | One-step lookahead on the adjacency graph, scoring each destination against the visible cards and next-Explore odds | Color the destination candidates by "hits next Build / next Ravage / P(next Explore)" |
| 13 | **Event choices** (Branch & Claw+; Invader Phase) | Compare option costs (energy, discards, forgetting) with their board effect | Event identity hidden until drawn; deterministic once revealed | Deterministic comparison after reveal | Same before/after delta as row 5 |
| 14 | **Reclaim timing** (Growth) | Reclaim now (lose this round's other growth) or wait? How many rounds until key cards come back? | Deterministic | Cycle-length planning (energy and card-play curve) | Timeline of when each card is next available |

**I-5. What the companion can say exactly vs. as odds.** Rows 3, 4, 6, 7 (this turn), 10, 13, and 14 are exact. Rows 2, 5, 7 (future), 11, and 12 need next-Explore odds. Rows 8 and 9 need deck odds or race projection. That supports the spec's split of stats into an invader forecast (mostly exact), a fear/victory race (projection), blight/loss risk (exact plus projection), and spirit economy (exact plus hypergeometric).

## Key Question 2: Precedent tools — what they compute, how they display it, perceived fairness, and learning effects

### Takeaway
The accepted precedents (Hearthstone Deck Tracker, Arkham chaos-bag calculators, Gloomhaven modifier simulators, Karsten's MTG mana tables, poker outs heuristics) compute odds only from public composition minus cards seen. They display those odds either as a live per-card percentage (trackers) or as static lookup tables and mental rules of thumb (Karsten, poker, Arkham's "I'm at −2"). Communities accept public-information trackers under a "pencil and paper" test. Where competition exists (online poker), they draw the line at real-time, situation-specific *advice*. No controlled evidence on learning effects was found for any of these tools; what exists is practitioner opinion on both sides.

### Cited Findings

**Hearthstone Deck Tracker (HDT) / HSReplay**
- HDT shows cards left in your deck, your hand and deck counts, and "draw chances." For the opponent it shows played cards, hand and deck count, and "the likelihood they have or will draw certain cards." — [HDT README](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/hearthsim/hearthstone-deck-tracker@master/README.md)
- The opponent hand probability "is based solely on the number of cards left in their deck and already in their hand." HDT enables Hearthstone's debug logging, "required to obtain any and all information." — [HDT FAQ wiki](https://github.com/HearthSim/Hearthstone-Deck-Tracker/wiki/FAQ)
- The HDT FAQ quotes Ben Brode: "any app that duplicates what you can do with a pencil and paper already is fine," and Zeriyah: "It's not against the TOS." — [HDT FAQ wiki](https://github.com/HearthSim/Hearthstone-Deck-Tracker/wiki/FAQ). No primary Blizzard source for the Brode quote was found. A German guide reports that Blizzard community managers disagreed with each other. — [mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/hearthstone-deck-tracker-tutorial,95526)
- On a 2015-era draft-pick overlay, Blizzard said: "Like any third party programs for Hearthstone, we can't condone or approve of their use. Our Terms of Use don't allow changing game files or automating game play." — [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/hearthstone-heroes-of-warcraft/hearthstone-arena-overlay-blizzard-say-we-can-t-condone-or-approve-of-their-use) (search snippet)
- HearthSim says the Hearthstone Tournament Player Handbook v2.6 §5.10 permits deck trackers "as long as tracker complies with the terms outlined in the player handbook" and the tournament doesn't forbid them. — [HearthSim help](https://help.hearthsim.net/en/articles/4345072-can-i-use-hearthstone-deck-tracker-in-an-official-hearthstone-tournament)
- Community dispute: one 2021 forum thread argues trackers give PC players an advantage over mobile players and that the pen-and-paper argument fails in practice. Replies cite Blizzard's pen-and-paper position. No staff replied. — [Blizzard EU forum, "Deck trackers give unfair advantage"](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/hearthstone/t/deck-trackers-give-unfair-advantage/6112). The German guide adds that there "simply wouldn't be enough time" to do it by hand in a real match. — [mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/hearthstone-deck-tracker-tutorial,95526) (search snippet)
- Learning-effect anecdotes, both directions: some players avoid trackers so they must track every card themselves and believe their skills improve. Another says their rank didn't change after adopting one. One felt manual tracking "takes up a lot of space for other thinking." — [Blizzard US forum, "Anyone playing this game without deck tracker?"](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/hearthstone/t/anyone-playing-this-game-without-deck-tracker/141972) (search snippet)

**Arkham Horror LCG chaos bag**
- The *Arkham Cards* app (unofficial) lets you "Draw tokens from the digital chaos bag, and calculate the odds of success before you do." — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1424000351)
- Curtis Miller's 2018 R simulator presents success probability as a table indexed by "I'm at X" (skill minus difficulty, −10 to +3), plus step-function plots comparing card effects. His group says "I'm at −2 for this test," a framing he finds intuitive and easy to translate into math. A finding: Olive McBride drops the success chance at −2 from 0.657 to 0.531. He also writes that poor human intuition about patterns ("you are more likely to find any pattern than no pattern at all") explains perceived "streaks" of auto-fails. — [R-bloggers, Miller 2018](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2018/08/evaluating-olive-mcbride-with-the-arkham-horror-lcg-chaos-bag-simulator-in-r/)

**Gloomhaven / Frosthaven attack-modifier deck**
- The `gloomhaven` Python package, an "Attack modifier deck simulator and probability calculator," presents CDF tables by base attack (base_attack_1 … 5) and summary rows (min, median, max, mean, std, mode). Example: base attack 1 has mean ≈ 1.06 and mode 0. It notes "The probablity calculations are non-trivial given the re-shuffling logic." — [libraries.io: gloomhaven](https://libraries.io/pypi/gloomhaven); repo [zachcoleman/gloomhaven](https://github.com/zachcoleman/gloomhaven)
- The reshuffle-triggering cards (null and x2) make simple static probability models misleading. — [Board Game Analysis, Jaws of the Lion perks](https://boardgameanalysis.com/gloomhaven-jaws-of-the-lion-which-perks-to-choose) (search snippet)

**Pandemic (cooperative; designer-sanctioned public-information tracking)**

**Poker: odds heuristics and the HUD/RTA line**
- Rule of 2 and 4: multiply outs by 4 with two cards to come and by 2 with one card to come. ### Inferences
- **Two display families.** (a) *Live per-item odds* (HDT draw chances, StS2 hover tooltips, Arkham Cards' "odds before you draw"). (b) *Static tables plus rules of thumb* (Karsten's mana tables, poker's rule of 2 and 4, Miller's "I'm at X" table). Family (b) is explicitly built to be internalized. Karsten calls his tables "rules of thumb" and his gold-card adjustment an "imprecise hack," and the poker heuristic is valued because players can do it at the table. For a learning goal, the companion should pair each live number with its family-(b) counterpart: the pocket rule ("1 ÷ unseen cards in this stage") and the exact value, so the player can check their mental estimate.
- **Assumptions shown as first-class content.** Karsten states his deck assumptions and conditioning. The Gloomhaven tool warns about reshuffle logic, and the StS2 mod warns about missing buffs. This matches the spec's `basis` string and `approx` flag. Displaying the basis is established practice, not an extra.
- **Framings that match how players already talk.** Arkham players' "I'm at −2" is a natural indexing for a lookup table. The Spirit Island counterparts are "this land is 4 damage, Defend 3 stops blight" and "Stage II, 3 cards unseen, Jungle is one of them."
- **The tools that draw the most fairness and learning criticism are the ones that recommend** (engine best move, poker RTA, situation-specific HUD stats). Tools that *count* draw much less. The companion's "show consequences and odds, not 'play this'" stance sits on the accepted side.
- **The Spirit Guide precedent.** The one Spirit Island companion found already does innate-threshold notifications, the exact element check (taxonomy row 4). That shows demand for the deterministic layer. No tool found does Ravage forecasting or Explore odds, so that part of the companion appears to be novel.

- No controlled study was found on whether deck trackers or odds overlays in games improve or harm player skill. All evidence is forum anecdote and coach opinion.

## Key Question 3: Learning design — which presentations build intuition, and the autopilot risk

### Takeaway
The research converges on four points. Showing an answer before the learner has thought about it invites over-reliance, and "think-first" designs reduce it at the cost of user preference. Failed attempts to answer before seeing the answer improve learning. Frequency formats and outcome-count visuals beat bare percentages for probabilistic reasoning and decisions. Guidance should fade as expertise grows, because full worked solutions can hinder experienced learners. Immediate feedback on one's own probability judgments improves calibration. Together these argue for a companion that leads with deltas and pocket rules, shows distributions as counts, and offers an optional "predict first" mode, even though the spec rejected predict-then-reveal as the v1 default.

### Cited Findings
- **Overreliance and cognitive forcing.** In a 199-participant study, three "cognitive forcing" designs "significantly reduced overreliance compared to the simple explainable AI approaches." But "people assigned the least favorable subjective ratings to the designs that reduced the overreliance the most," and the benefit was larger for people high in Need for Cognition. Published in Proc. ACM HCI 5 (CSCW1), April 2021. — [Buçinca, Malaya, Gajos, arXiv 2102.09692](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692); [Harvard project page](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml). One forcing design asks people to decide before they see the AI's recommendation. — (search snippet, same sources)
- **Automation complacency and bias.** A review finds that complacency and automation bias are overlapping, attention-driven effects. Complacency appears under multi-task load, affects both novices and experts, and "does not disappear with simple practice." — [Parasuraman & Manzey 2010, Human Factors 52(3)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0018720810376055); [open copy, TU Berlin](https://depositonce.tu-berlin.de/items/0c787d91-6021-480b-a333-009e0d1d0092/full) (search snippets)
- **Pretesting and generation.** Unsuccessful retrieval attempts enhance later learning (six experiments, with test vs. read-only conditions). — [Kornell, Hays & Bjork 2009, JEP:LMC 35(4)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19586265/) (search snippet). Failed tests followed by *immediate* feedback beat presentation-only. Delayed feedback gave different results. — [Hays, Kornell & Bjork 2012 (preprint)](https://sites.lifesci.ucla.edu/psych-bjorklab/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Hays_Kornell_RBjork_inpress.pdf) (search snippet)
- **Worked examples, fading, expertise reversal.** Worked examples beat problem solving early in skill acquisition, but "this advantage disappeared over time" (expertise reversal). Fading, which gradually turns example steps into problems, prompts self-explanation. Adaptive fading beat fixed fading, which beat pure problem solving. — [Wikipedia: Worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect); [Kalyuga et al., expertise reversal in tutored problem solving](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/the-expertise-reversal-effect-and-worked-examples-in-tutored-prob/); [Renkl/Atkinson-line paper (ASU)](https://faculty.engineering.asu.edu/mre/wp-content/uploads/sites/31/2020/02/Exp_Rev_LI06.pdf) (search snippets)
- **Natural frequencies.** In Gigerenzer & Hoffrage (1995), "the percentage of correct (i.e., Bayesian) inferences tripled, specifically, from 16 to 46%" when information was given as natural frequencies rather than probabilities. — [Hoffrage, Hafenbrädl & Bouquet 2015, Frontiers in Psychology 6:642](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00642/full)
- **Distribution displays for decisions.** In an incentivized transit-decision experiment, quantile dotplots (50 outcomes) reached "97% of optimal" expected payoff (95% CI 95–98%), 5 points above a no-uncertainty control, with lower within-subject variability. CDF plots "performed nearly as well," and "both outperformed textual uncertainty," which "was sensitive to the probability interval communicated." CHI 2018, Best Paper Honorable Mention. — [Fernandes, Walls, Munson, Hullman & Kay 2018](https://mucollective.northwestern.edu/project/uncertainty-bus)
- **Calibration training.** In the IARPA forecasting tournament, a probability-training module (reference classes, averaging heuristics) beat scenario training, which beat no training, and training improved both calibration and resolution. — [APS Observer summary of Mellers et al. 2014](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/observer/obsonline/predicting-uncertain-events-on-a-global-scale.html); [Knowledge at Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/how-to-improve-the-science-of-forecasting/) (search snippets). A later item-response-theory reanalysis argued these training effects were overstated. — reported in the same search; the specific source URL was not isolated (see Gaps).
- **Calibration tool precedent.** Clearer Thinking's *Calibrate Your Judgment* (funded by Open Philanthropy) has users make probabilistic predictions on thousands of questions, gives a score immediately after each one, and tracks progress over time. Its scoring rules are published. — [Clearer Thinking launch post, Oct 2019](https://www.clearerthinking.org/post/2019/10/16/practice-making-accurate-predictions-with-our-new-tool); [Greenberg, "Calibration Scoring Rules for Practical Prediction Training," arXiv 1808.07501](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1808.07501) (search snippets)
- **Practitioner analog: think first, engine second.** Chess coaches advise replaying a game with the evaluation bar hidden, finding the mistake yourself, and only then asking the engine a specific question. — [Payline Data guide](https://paylinedata.com/blog/online-chess) (search snippet); [Randjelovic, Chess.com](https://www.chess.com/blog/mizant/when-the-engine-makes-you-worse-at-chess). Lichess built this into "Learn from your mistakes." — [lichess blog](https://lichess.org/blog/WFvLpiQAACMA8e9D/learn-from-your-mistakes) (search snippet)

### Inferences
- **Autopilot risk.** An always-on dashboard (spec Section 2) is exactly the condition where Parasuraman & Manzey find complacency: Brett plays Steam (the manual task) while the companion runs on a second monitor (the automated aid). Buçinca's result suggests the most effective counter (decide first, then see the numbers) will feel worse. That matches the spec's rejection of predict-then-reveal for v1. A low-friction compromise: an optional "hold" toggle or keybind that blurs the forecast panel until Brett commits a guess, with a running calibration score on those guesses in the Clearer Thinking style. Off by default, so it stays consistent with the v1 decision.
- **Lead with deltas, not verdicts.** Showing "what changes if you hit land X" (taxonomy row 5) exposes the mechanism, the worked-example ingredient, without ranking plays. Ranking plays is the engine-bar failure mode Randjelovic describes.
- **Probabilities as counts.** "2 of the 3 unseen Stage II cards explore here" (a natural frequency) should beat "67%" for building intuition (Gigerenzer & Hoffrage). Where outcomes vary, as in multi-round blight projection, a quantile-dotplot-style "20 simulated futures" strip or a CDF beats a single expected value or a text interval (Fernandes et al.).
- **Fade the scaffolding.** Expertise reversal suggests the companion should let Brett collapse the explanations (basis strings, pocket rules) per stat once he consistently predicts them. Showing everything forever risks redundancy cost for an experienced player.
- **Pocket rules beside exact values** (from Key Question 2's Karsten and poker precedents) give a built-in self-test: Brett can do the mental estimate first and use the exact value as immediate feedback, which is the mechanism behind the pretesting and calibration findings.

### Gaps
- No study was found that directly tests odds overlays in games, such as deck trackers, for effects on learning or skill transfer.
- The URL of the IRT reanalysis challenging Mellers et al. was not isolated, and the effect sizes of Mellers et al. 2014 were not retrieved.
- The Coefficient Giving / Open Philanthropy calibration post returned 403. A claim seen in search, that a 2004 Russo review found feedback "substantially enhanced" calibration, is unverified.
- The names of Buçinca's three forcing interventions were not confirmed from fetched text.

## Key Question 4: Fair-play boundaries — public-information tracking vs. cheating, especially in solo and co-op play

### Takeaway
The working line across communities is that a tool may automate what a player could do with pencil and paper from public information: counting seen cards, tallying discards, computing odds over the unseen remainder. It may not reveal hidden state (deck order, face-down cards, RNG), modify the game, or automate play. In competitive online poker the line is stricter: situation-specific, real-time analysis is banned while the client is open, even if it uses only public information. In co-op games, designers sometimes endorse public-information aids outright (Pandemic). For a solo Spirit Island companion, "fairness" means faithfulness to the tabletop information state, which is exactly the spec's firewall.

### Cited Findings
- **Pencil-and-paper test (Hearthstone).** "any app that duplicates what you can do with a pencil and paper already is fine." Attributed to Ben Brode and quoted in [HDT's FAQ](https://github.com/HearthSim/Hearthstone-Deck-Tracker/wiki/FAQ). HDT's opponent odds use only card counts. — [same](https://github.com/HearthSim/Hearthstone-Deck-Tracker/wiki/FAQ)
- **Blizzard's outer boundary.** "Our Terms of Use don't allow changing game files or automating game play." — [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/hearthstone-heroes-of-warcraft/hearthstone-arena-overlay-blizzard-say-we-can-t-condone-or-approve-of-their-use) (search snippet). The HSReplay overlay FAQ says it "only reads publicly available game log files." — [HSReplay Battlegrounds overlay FAQ](https://blue.hsreplay.net/battlegrounds/overlay/) (search snippet)
- **Co-op designer endorsement.** Pandemic lets players examine both discard piles at any time "Because Pandemic is a test of cooperation and mettle (and not of memory)," while hands stay unshown in Normal and Heroic games. — [Pandemic rulebook](https://aadl.org/files/catalog_guides/Pandemic_Rules.pdf)
- **Spirit Island's own hidden zones.** Earned Fear cards go "face-down" into the Earned Fear Cards area and are revealed only when resolved in the Fear step. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook). Invader cards are chosen "without looking at them," and one per stage goes back in the box unseen. — [RulesPal](https://rulespal.com/spirit-island/rulebook); [Dized](https://rules.dized.com/game/_7rPptSyRn6_unBi4-2eVg/sjhFSW3wTyKIdbBw1Uc9sg/how-do-i-create-an-invader-deck)
- **Online poker's stricter line.** HUDs are numeric-only, with no situation-specific dynamic stats, and equity or situation analyzers are banned while the client is open. — [PokerNews 2015](https://www.pokernews.com/news/2015/10/pokerstars-announces-changes-to-third-party-tools-policy-22920.htm). Real-time assistance is prohibited and approved tools are whitelisted. — [DeucesCracked](https://www.deucescracked.com/blog/best-poker-hud-software-2026-pokerstars-vs-ggpoker-compliance) (search snippet)
- **Access asymmetry as a fairness complaint** (competitive play only): PC players with trackers vs. mobile players without. — [Blizzard EU forum](https://eu.forums.blizzard.com/en/hearthstone/t/deck-trackers-give-unfair-advantage/6112)
- **Project's own rule.** The companion "shows only information that a player at a physical table, playing the same game, could know at that moment," with odds from "public rules plus cards already seen — never from the save's hidden contents." It is enforced by permutation-invariance tests. `log.txt` contains hidden card names (for example, Fear cards when earned), so raw log events never leave the decoder. — [Steam companion spec, Section 5](/home/brett/repos/spirit-island-steam-companion/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-steam-companion-design.md)

### Inferences
- **"Reads only public log files" is not sufficient.** HSReplay's compliance claim rests on the log being public. The spec documents that Spirit Island's `log.txt` names hidden Fear cards and the save holds deck order and RNG. The fairness test must look at the *content* of what is shown, not the *channel* it came from. The spec's invariance test (permuting hidden identities within look-alike groups must leave all output byte-identical) is a stricter, testable version of the pencil-and-paper principle. No precedent tool found states its fairness guarantee this rigorously.
- **For solo and co-op, the competitive fairness questions (opponent advantage, access asymmetry) mostly drop out.** The remaining question is integrity of the learning signal: an odds number derived from hidden state would teach a false sense of what is knowable at the table. That reason holds independently of rules or etiquette.
- **The poker RTA line suggests a self-imposed second boundary** separate from information access: the companion should show consequences and odds, not recommendations. Recommending plays would be the solo analog of poker's banned situation analyzers. This also aligns with the learning findings in Key Question 3.
- **Pandemic's rationale ("not a test of memory") is the cleanest precedent** for the companion's card-counting features (seen invader cards per stage, discards, Fear count). Spirit Island's face-down earned Fear cards and unseen removed Invader cards are the designer-intended hidden zones the companion must never pierce.

### Gaps
- No official Greater Than Games or Handelabra statement was found on third-party helper tools or on card counting in Spirit Island.
- No community discussion (BGG or Reddit) was found on whether odds tools count as "cheating" in solo or co-op board games specifically. Two searches turned up nothing relevant.
- The current official PokerStars and GGPoker tool-policy pages were not fetched; current policy rests on secondary sources.
